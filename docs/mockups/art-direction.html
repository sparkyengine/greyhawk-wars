Original art proposal

One set of silhouettes, three ways to dress them

Everything below is drawn from scratch as vector art, and the page builds it in code. Pick a look with the switch at the top and the whole kit changes. The silhouettes stay the same, so switching styles later costs almost nothing.

How it differs from the 1991 components
  • Round or hex tokens with a bust or gear silhouette, not square counters with full figures.
  • New heraldry for every nation. No copied shields.
  • Terrain drawn from generic motifs: conifers, domes, ridges, reeds, dunes.
  • Two-step units show damage on the token itself, with pips and a crack.
1 · Troop tokens

24 unit tokens

Rim color and a small glyph give the alignment: a sun for Good, a ring for Neutral, a star for Evil. The glyph keeps it readable for colorblind players. The seal shows current strength. Click a unit with a second side to see it wounded.

2 · Terrain

Seven terrains, each with its own motif

Each tile shows what it costs to enter, so the picture teaches the rule. The same motifs are scattered across traced map areas, so the art follows whatever area shapes we trace.

4 · Boats

Three hulls, plus a damaged state

Nations can use different hulls: the longship for the north, the cog for traders and the Sea Barons, the galley for the south. All three count as the same Ship counter in the rules. Damaged ships lose their sail.

Recommendation

Start with A, Ink and vellum. It reads best at small sizes, needs the fewest extra assets and fits a tabletop that is mostly map. Borrow B's brass rims for hero and special tokens if you want a little glow.

What is a sketch, what is final

These silhouettes are concept art generated in code. They are good enough to test readability and style. Final art would be refined by hand per unit, still as vector files, so tokens stay crisp at any zoom.

Next steps

Pick a style. I would then refine the 24 silhouettes, draw the cards' spot art, and wire the kit into the map renderer as a single SVG sprite sheet.
